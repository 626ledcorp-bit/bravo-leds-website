What do lumens mean?

Lumens measure total light output — more lumens, more light. Our listings show lumens per pair (both bulbs together). As a rough guide: a stock halogen low beam puts out around 1,000–1,500 lumens per bulb, while our LED lines range from about 6,000 lumens per pair on the entry tier up to 26,000 on the flagship Platinum 4070 series. Lumens tell you brightness; the beam pattern of your housing decides where that light goes.

What's the difference between 6000K, 8000K, and 3000K?

That's color temperature, not brightness — higher K is not brighter, just a different color:

  • 6000K — crisp cool white, the classic LED look. Most popular for low/high beams and reverse.
  • 8000K — icy white with a blue tint. Style choice; slightly less usable light than 6000K.
  • 3000K — golden yellow. The foul-weather pick for fog lights — yellow cuts through rain and fog better than white.

If you want maximum usable light, 6000K is the sweet spot. If you want fog lights that actually work in fog, go 3000K.

Why is my turn signal blinking so fast after installing LEDs?

That's hyperflash. LEDs draw far less current than the incandescent bulb they replaced, so your car thinks a bulb burned out and blinks double-time to warn you. The fix is a load resistor wired in parallel on each turn-signal circuit (mounted to bare metal — they get hot), or a CANbus-ready bulb/decoder that needs no splicing. See our turn signal install guide.

Do I need a CANbus decoder or anti-flicker harness?

Maybe — it depends on the car. If your new LEDs flicker, strobe, stay dimly lit when off, or trigger a "bulb out" dash warning, your car's computer is fighting the lower power draw. A plug-and-play CANbus decoder inline with the bulb usually solves it in seconds. Most older cars need nothing at all.
